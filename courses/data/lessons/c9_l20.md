The previous lesson documented the doctrine applied to enemies. This one documents the doctrine applied to grain — and it matters to the course's architecture that these are the same years, the same government, and at bottom the same idea. War Communism, the economic regime of roughly mid-1918 through early 1921, was the first attempt in history to run a large country on the actual program of Unit 1: money withering, trade abolished as speculation, the market replaced by requisition and allocation, the peasant's harvest treated as state property that the peasant merely happened to be standing near. Its apologists then and since have read the name backward — a *war* economy, improvised under siege, no more ideological than British rationing. The record this lesson assembles says otherwise: the policy outran every military need, was celebrated by its authors in real time as communism arriving ahead of schedule, was enforced by the previous lesson's instruments against the very classes the revolution claimed as its own, and ended in the largest famine Europe had seen in a generation — after which the survivors' verdict was delivered not by emigres but by the regime's own sailors, and the founder's response was a retreat he had the candor to call one. The sequence — program, catastrophe, revolt, retreat — is the course's first complete experimental run, and its result stands as evidence for everything Unit 2 argued about calculation, incentives, and knowledge.

## Requisition

The instrument at the center was *prodrazvyorstka* — grain requisitioning. From May 1918, a state grain monopoly; armed detachments from the cities, joined by Committees of the Poor deputized to split the village against itself, assessed each district's "surplus" and took it at nominal prices in worthless currency — which is to say, took it. The definition of surplus was administrative, not agronomic: quotas were set to the state's need, and where the harvest fell short of the quota, the difference came out of the peasant's seed corn and his children's bread. Village resistance was met under the previous lesson's rubrics — hostages, punitive columns, the category "kulak" stretched to cover whoever resisted. Around this core the rest of the program assembled: private trade criminalized (the "bagmen" who smuggled sacks of flour to the starving cities were speculators by statute and lifeline in fact), industry nationalized to the workshop level, labor militarized, money printed into oblivion with theorists explaining that hyperinflation was the euthanasia of the ruble and good riddance. Bukharin and Preobrazhensky's *The ABC of Communism* (1919) presented the system not as siege expedient but as the thing itself taking shape. The course flags the point because it decides the lesson's argument: when the measures are the ideology, the ideology owns the results.

The results were what Unit 2's incentive analysis predicts without needing hindsight. A peasant whose surplus is confiscated grows no surplus: sown area contracted season by season, livestock was eaten rather than surrendered, and the yield that in a market year would have been insurance became, under requisition, evidence. By 1920 agricultural output stood at a fraction of prewar levels and industrial output lower still; the cities emptied as workers fled to relatives with land. The state answered shortfall the only way requisition can — by requisitioning harder — and the countryside answered in kind. By late 1920, with the Whites beaten and the "war" half of the name expired, peasant war engulfed whole provinces: western Siberia, the Volga, and above all Tambov, where Alexander Antonov's rising fielded tens of thousands. The suppression orders survive: Order No. 171 (June 1921) prescribed hostage-taking and the shooting of eldest sons for hidden weapons; Tukhachevsky's command authorized poison gas against forest encampments; whole villages were deported to the first camps. The reader is asked to hold the plain description in view: two years after the civil war was won, the workers' and peasants' state was conducting chemical-assisted counterinsurgency against peasants over grain.

## Kronstadt

Then the verdict from inside. In March 1921 the sailors of the Kronstadt naval base — the October revolution's own praetorians, "the pride and glory of the revolution" in Trotsky's earlier phrase — rose with a program that no White general could have written: freely elected soviets, freedom of speech and press *for socialists and anarchists*, the release of socialist political prisoners, an end to requisition detachments, the peasant's right to his own produce. It was the revolution's stated program, quoted back at its owners, and that is precisely what made it unanswerable by argument. The regime called the sailors White agents (the archives have yielded no such direction), and sent infantry across the March ice under Tukhachevsky; the base fell after days of assault, with hundreds executed afterward and thousands imprisoned or fled to Finland. The episode earns its place in this unit not for its scale but for its diagnostic purity, and the course states the finding carefully: when the movement's most loyal constituency demanded the movement's own promises, the movement classified the demand as counter-revolution and machine-gunned it. Legitimacy-by-proletariat had met an actual proletariat, and the tiebreaker was the Cheka. Lenin drew the lesson with his usual clarity, telling the Tenth Party Congress — meeting as the ice assault went in — that the lesson of Kronstadt was the need for more unity and discipline; the same congress banned factions *inside* the Party. The circle of permitted opinion, having contracted from society to class to party, now contracted to the leadership. L19's doctrine had completed its geometry.

## The Famine

The bill came due in the countryside. Drought struck the Volga basin in 1920 and 1921 — and the course states at once, because its method requires it, that the drought was real and no government's fault. What policy contributed was the removal of every buffer a peasant economy keeps against drought: reserves confiscated in successive requisitions, seed grain taken, sown area shrunken by the incentive collapse, draft animals dead, and the private trade that historically moved grain toward hunger a crime. Famine on this preparation was not a risk but a schedule. Across 1921–22 the hunger zone covered the Volga, the southern Urals, and southern Ukraine; the demographic reckonings put excess deaths around five million, with serious estimates spanning roughly four to six million or more — the century's first man-assisted famine, and not its last appearance in this unit.[^1] Two features of the response belong in the record. First, the regime permitted — after costly weeks of hesitation, and on the appeal of Gorky rather than the government — the largest foreign relief operation in history to that date: Herbert Hoover's American Relief Administration, which at peak was feeding on the order of ten million people a day. The course notes the fact against itself: the capitalist republic's food, moved by the price system's logistics, fed the communist experiment's victims, and Soviet historiography spent decades minimizing it. Second, the founder's use of the emergency: Lenin's secret letter of March 1922, published only after the archives opened, directs that the Shuya resistance to church-valuables confiscation be answered with maximal executions precisely *because* the famine made the moment opportune — the starving countryside, he reasoned, could not defend the clergy. The document is in the same file drawer as the Penza telegram, and it closes the question of whether the famine softened the doctrine.

What the famine and the risings did force was the retreat. The same Tenth Congress replaced requisition with a tax in kind; by steps, private trade and small manufacture were re-legalized as the New Economic Policy, and within two seasons the market did what requisition never had — the cities were fed, sown area recovered, the currency was stabilized on a gold-backed ruble. The course enters NEP as testimony: the founder's own government, to survive, restored precisely the mechanisms the doctrine had abolished, and recovery followed the restoration. Lenin called it a retreat and meant it tactically; the honest reading is an experimental result.

## The Honest Counterpoint

The defense case, at full strength. War Communism arose amid genuine collapse — the tsarist economy was failing before October, the world war had wrecked transport, and *every* combatant managed grain (the Provisional Government itself declared a monopoly in March 1917); requisition had precedents, not only Bolshevik logic. The drought was real; famine had struck the same provinces under the tsars in 1891 with a response hardly nobler. The regime did ultimately admit the ARA, did adopt NEP, and some in the Party had urged the tax-in-kind a year earlier — evidence of a system capable of correction. All granted, and none of it survives the audit. The precedent defense fails on degree and intent: no other belligerent abolished trade, criminalized the market, or glossed its controls as the arrival of a new civilization — the measures exceeded the war and were theorized as exceeding it. The 1891 comparison fails on the ledger: the tsarist famine, met by a weaker state, killed a fraction of 1921's total, and no tsarist minister is on record scheduling executions *because* the peasants were too hungry to resist. And the correction defense proves the course's point rather than the apologist's: the system corrected only when correction meant *suspending the doctrine* — the feedback that saved the regime came from markets, mutiny, and American charity, none of which the theory has a category for. What the retreat preserved, moreover, was the part the next lessons need: the party monopoly, the police, the category enemy — the whole of L19's toolkit, warehoused through the NEP truce. The founder died in January 1924 with the truce holding. The heir who ended it regarded the retreat as unfinished business with the peasantry, and settled it. To collectivization, and the terror-famine.
